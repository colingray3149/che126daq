   In the pasts four months in Los Angeles County, residents were evacuated because of fires at two
   facilities with a large number of lithium-ion battery arrays:

    1. In Boyle Heights in mid-June 2026
        (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Boyle_Heights_warehouse_fire) , a fire started on the roof of a
        cold storage hub, and "air monitors detected hydrogen fluoride, a toxic gas linked to
        burning lithium-ion batteries    (https://www.dankolaw.com/blog/boyle-heights-warehouse-fire/)
        ." Facilities like this one may use lithium-ion batteries for forklifts and other equipment inside the
        warehouse as well as for large rooftop solar systems to provide emergency backup and for "peak
        shaving."
    2. In Laverne the week before the Fall 2026 term started            (https://ktla.com/news/local-news/la-
       verne-battery-fire-evacuation-update/) , a fire ignited at a small Battery Energy Storage Systems
       (BESS) associated with solar panels at a municipal water treatment plant.

   In these situations, first responders took multiple actions to prevent thermal runaway. For instance,
   for the Laverne fire, firefighters used 1800 gal/min of water to stabilize battery temperature,
   according to local news station KTLA            (https://abc7.com/post/battery-unit-exposed-fire-
   metropolitan-water-district-facility-la-verne-continues-heating-evacuations-remain-place/19867853/) .

   According to the Electric Power Research Institute (EPRI) database of Stationary Energy
   Storage (SES) Failure Incidents        (https://energystorage.epri.com/hub/public/en/failure-incident-
   database.html#stationary_energy_storage_failure_incidents) , there have been at least 54 events for
   lithium-ion batteries in the past 10 years. This SES Failure Incidents database has ~68% of its events
   with an unknown root cause, because thermal runaway generates extreme heat (often exceeding
   1,000°C), which destroys physical evidence and local hardware. (NOTE: The database is incomplete.
   Plus, a separate EPRI database focuses on temporary storage, manufacturing, and mobile sources.)

   Because of these incidents, the National Fire Protection Association first published "NFPA 855:
   Standard for the Installation of Stationary Energy Storage Systems" in 2020, with the most recent
   code update in 2026.
