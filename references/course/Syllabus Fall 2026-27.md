 GOAL
 Using the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) design process and other good design practices over a 10-week
 period, each person will help their group design, build, test and modify a proof-of-concept to prevent or
 mitigate health & environmental impact of fires at a facility with a lithium-ion battery arrays. During the
 process, individuals communicate status, results and recommendations for improved design and/or
 components for a feasibility stage.


 LEARNING OBJECTIVES
 At the conclusion of ChE126, you should be able to do the following:

     Follow a formalized design process, and explain its various stages.
     Ideate multiple options before (a) evaluating them and (b) ultimately selecting a suitable solution,
     while understanding how constraints and requirements affect that decision.

 GRADES
 40% Laboratory/Design Work: Individual Grade. Effective work, using the PDCA design cycle and
 other design tools & methods, as needed, after learning them; pre-lab preparation.

 5% Laboratory Safety: Group Grade. Environmental health, safety & cleanliness at island and hoods.

 10% Design Documents (5% Requirements, 2.5% FMEA, 2.5% Drawings): Group Grade. At the end of
 the term, each group submits a single electronic document for requirements, Failure Modes and Effects
 Analysis (FMEA), and drawings. All group members are expected to contribute throughout the term.

 17.5% Design Presentation: Individual Grade. During Finals Week, each student will present (max. 15
 min) about their proof-of-concept product.

     Section 1 (MF 9a-Noon) has presentations scheduled for Tue Dec 8 from 830-1130a in Spalding 102
     (nine students)
     Section 2 (MF 1-4p) has presentations scheduled for Wed Dec 9 from 330-630p (three students)
     Section 3 (MF Noon-1p & 2-4p) has presentations scheduled for Fri Dec 11 from 330-630p (two
     students), which can be combined with Section 2 if all students agree

 0% Short Presentation: During Week 10, each student will meet with Dr. Vicic to present four slides in a
 one-to-one environment. You will receive immediate feedback to help you further improve your
 presentation skills and slides. You will use these four slides – maybe modified – in your design
 presentation. Meetings are usually 15 minutes in duration during lab.

 5% Presentation Practice: Individual Grade. During Weeks 6 & 8, you will complete various short
 activities using an online system to help you improve certain aspects of your verbal presentation skill.
 This exercise is intended to help with your design memo, short presentation, and design presentation.

 OTHER NOTES
     Absences: If you are going to miss a lab session because of illness, interview, etc., please inform Dr.
     Vicic beforehand. Makeup labs and/or makeup work outside of lab will be scheduled on a case-by-
     case basis.
     Collaboration: Each assignment has its own guidelines for collaboration. For general lab work, you
     should share everything freely within your group, including data, files, detailed designs, code, etc.
     Between groups, you should feel free to discuss general ideas and concepts, but do not share exact
     designs, files, or code. For instance, with other groups you can discuss freely how you might modify
     a surface and the reasons why, but you should not share exact dimensions, distances, drawings,
     procedures, or component choices.
     Generative AI/LLMs: Each assignment has its own guidelines for generative AI use.
     Resources: We will provide you a few of the resources (e.g., access to standard lab glassware, etc.;
     multimeters & other items to measure performance) that you might need to get started. In most cases
     you will need to search for an item that meets the requirements for that subsystem/component and
     submit your order to Dr. Vicic.
     Budget: In addition to the equipment we provide, each group may spend up to $650 for items
     relevant for the project. Submit requests to Dr. Vicic, who will approve and order the items – or
     provide feedback about alternatives.
