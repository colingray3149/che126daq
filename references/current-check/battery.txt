12USE IT RIGHT
Keep it inside its ratings.
What this cell is, and how to use it within its ratings.
This is a bare cell: a component for protected packs and devices built for it. It is not a standalone consumer battery and not for vaping.
Flat top, unprotected. Check that your device takes a flat-top cell up to 65.00 mm long.
Charge on a lithium-ion (Li-ion) charger that stops at 4.20 V. Stop discharging at 2.5 V [1].
Stay inside the ratings and their conditions: 20 A maximum continuous at room temperature, with pack protection at no more than 70 °C cell surface; charge at up to 4 A between 0 and 45 °C [1].
Replace a torn wrap before use. Carry cells in a case, never loose.
For packs: matched cells of one model and lot, a BMS, fusing, and a way for heat to leave.

13QUESTIONS
Questions, answered.
Short answers. Where an answer comes from a document, it carries its source; the rest is standard handling practice. Anything else: call 1-800-547-3050 or text 1-833-419-2192.
Is the 25R a protected cell?
No. It is a bare flat-top cell with no protection circuit. Use it only in packs with a battery management system (BMS) or in devices built for unprotected cells.
What does “20 A max. continuous, at room temperature” mean?
It is the specification’s maximum continuous discharge, rated at room temperature, 23 ± 3 °C [1]. The same spec asks for pack protection that ends discharge by 70 °C at the cell surface. In Samsung’s 2013 test, one cell discharged at 20 A reached about 96 °C by the end [2], so a protected pack would stop near 1,314 mAh. In a pack, plan for less than 20 A per cell.
Why do some listings quote a much higher current?
Samsung’s 2013 presentation prints a momentary pulse figure, and some listings repeat it as if it were a rating. We don’t list pulse figures. The current specification rates 20 A maximum continuous at room temperature [1], so 20 A is the discharge figure we publish.
What is its typical capacity?
The current specification prints minimums only: 2,500 mAh at 0.2C and 2,450 mAh at 10 A [1]. Samsung’s 2013 presentation prints typical figures of about 2,560 mAh at 0.2C and 2,539 mAh at 10 A [2]. In its discharge chart the cell delivered about 2,561 mAh at 10 A.
How fast can I charge it?
The spec rates a 4 A charge (62 min) and a 1.25 A standard charge (180 min), both CC-CV to 4.20 V, between 0 and 45 °C ambient [1]. It doesn’t label any current as a maximum, so we don’t suggest charging faster than 4 A.
What charger should I use?
A lithium-ion charger with a CC-CV profile (constant current, then constant voltage) that stops at 4.20 V. Not a NiMH, LiFePO4 or lead-acid charger.
How long will it run at my current?
Runtime ≈ capacity ÷ current. From Samsung’s curves: about 1 h 00 min at 2.5 A, 15 min at 10 A and 7.6 min at 20 A, from full at 23 °C ambient (figure 3.7). In a pack with 70 °C protection, a 20 A discharge may stop sooner.
How does cold affect it?
In Samsung’s 2013 test at 10 A, the cell delivered about 2,206 mAh to 2.5 V at 0 °C and about 2,030 mAh at −20 °C, against about 2,561 mAh at 23 °C [2]. The spec rates discharge from −20 °C and charging only from 0 °C ambient [1].
How many cycles will it last?
The spec rates at least 1,500 mAh left after 250 cycles of 4 A charge and 20 A discharge [1]. In Samsung’s 2013 test of that program the cell had about 1,756 mAh at cycle 250 [2]. Neither document gives an end-of-life figure. Heat, current, depth of discharge and storage all change the result.
Can I use it in a vape or e-cigarette?
No. It is a bare cell: a component for protected packs and devices built for it, not for vaping.
Can I solder to it?
Don’t solder directly to the cell: the heat can damage the seal and internal parts. Spot-weld nickel strip, or use a holder.
Can I mix 25R cells with other cells?
No, not in the same pack. Use cells of the same model and lot, at the same state of charge. In series, the weakest cell reaches 2.5 V first and limits the whole pack, and mismatched cells drift further apart with every cycle.
How should I store spare cells?
In a case, away from metal objects, somewhere dry and cool. The spec allows 0–23 °C for up to a year [1]; the SDS suggests about 20 °C at 20–60 % charge, about 3.6–3.9 V [3]. Check the voltage every few months.
